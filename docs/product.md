# Product and principles

## Why we are building Chalkline

General-purpose diagramming tools let people draw systems, but their shapes do not understand the components they represent. Chalkline pairs a collaborative whiteboard with structured architecture data. This gives teams a place to build and revise a system together, then receive feedback tied to the actual design.

Our three goals are:

1. Make something cool as hell.
2. Build job-ready skills that strengthen resumes and applications.
3. Gain a real user base and GitHub stars.

The first goal asks us to build a product we are proud to show and use. The second asks every engineer and designer to own work they can explain in interviews. The third asks us to test the product with people beyond the project team and learn from their use.

## Intended experience

Users can create a board on an infinite canvas, add typed components, connect them, and invite collaborators through a shareable link. The initial catalog covers clients, DNS, CDNs, load balancers, reverse proxies, API gateways, stateless services, databases, caches, queues, streams, and object storage. Live cursors and presence show who is working on the board. Boards are saved centrally and recover after a disconnect.

The semantic data enables structural validation. Guided exercises and richer feedback could build on that foundation as the product matures. See the [roadmap](roadmap.md) for directions the team will explore.

## Principles

- **Semantic before intelligent.** Structured components and connections must be reliable before AI reviews a design.
- **Collaboration before simulation.** The shared editor and graph model come before advanced analysis.
- **Real ownership.** Subteams get clear goals and constraints, with room to research and own implementation decisions.
- **Responsible AI-assisted engineering.** Contributors must understand, test, review, and maintain what they merge.
- **Build the learning-intensive parts.** The team owns synchronization and deployment because both provide valuable engineering experience at this scale.
